Q2 (20 marks) — SQL_DDL_DML
Consider the following relations in a library management system: 

Book (bookID: char(5), title: varchar(100), author: varchar(50), genre: varchar(30)) 
Member (memberID: char(5), name: varchar(50), phone: char(10), address: varchar(100)) 
Loan (loanID: int, bookID: char(5), memberID: char(5), loanDate: datetime, returnDate: datetime, status: varchar(10)) 
Fine (fineID: int, loanID: int, amount: real, paymentStatus: varchar(10)) 

The database stores information about books available in the library in the Book table, which includes a unique book ID, title, author, and genre. The Member table keeps track of library members, including their unique ID, name, phone number, and address. The Loan table records details of book loans, including a unique loan ID, the ID of the book borrowed, the ID of the member who borrowed it, the date of the loan, the return date, and the status of the loan. The Fine table records any fines associated with loans, including a unique fine ID, the loan ID, the fine amount, and the payment status.

Use SQL queries to answer the following questions:

i. List the names and phone numbers of members who have borrowed the book titled 'The Great Gatsby'. (4 marks)

ii. Identify the member who has incurred the highest total fine amount and display their member ID and address. (4 marks)

iii. Retrieve the titles of books currently on loan along with the names of the members who have borrowed them. (4 marks)

b) Create a function that calculates the total fine amount for a given member, considering a late penalty of 10% for overdue fines. (4 marks)

c) Develop a trigger that updates the total fine amount in the Member table whenever a new fine is added or an existing fine is updated, ensuring accurate reflection of any partial payments made by members. (4 marks)
